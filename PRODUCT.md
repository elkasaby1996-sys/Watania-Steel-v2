# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Watania Steel operations staff use this system across dispatch, order management, management oversight, warehouse and inventory work, driver administration, and daily operational review. Most users work on desktop computers during active operations, with a smaller group checking or updating information from iPhone and Android phones.

Users need a simple, focused view of what is happening today: current orders, delivery progress, drivers, tonnage, inventory and offcut signals, and the operational exceptions that need attention.

## Product Purpose

This product is an internal ERP and order tracking system for Watania Steel. It helps the team understand today's work, manage orders and drivers, track steel-related operational data, and review daily performance snapshots without digging through disconnected records.

Success means the team can quickly answer: what is happening today, what has changed, what is delayed, who is responsible, and what operational snapshot should guide the next decision.

## Brand Personality

Industrial, calm, precise.

The interface should feel grounded in steel operations: clear, sturdy, and practical. It should reduce noise, show the current state with confidence, and avoid theatrical styling. The tone is direct and operational, with enough polish to feel trustworthy but no decorative excess.

## Anti-references

Do not make the product feel like a generic template, a flashy SaaS dashboard, or an overly colorful analytics product. Avoid decorative chart palettes, generic metric-card grids, marketing-style hero layouts, and visual noise that competes with today's operational priorities.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with today: every primary screen should make current operational state easier to understand before historical or secondary detail.
2. Prioritize scan speed: dense information is acceptable when hierarchy, grouping, and labels make it quick to parse.
3. Keep the system calm: use restrained emphasis so exceptions, delays, and required actions stand out without turning the UI into an alarm panel.
4. Make operations traceable: users should be able to move from snapshot to detail without losing context.
5. Design for working conditions: desktop workflows should be efficient, while mobile views should preserve essential checks and updates.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The product should support Arabic, English, and Hindi usage patterns, including copy length variation and future right-to-left layout needs for Arabic. Desktop is the primary environment, with responsive support for iPhone and Android phones.

Status colors, charts, and alerts should not rely on color alone. Keep contrast high enough for day-to-day operational environments, use clear labels for important state changes, and avoid motion that distracts from repeated work.
